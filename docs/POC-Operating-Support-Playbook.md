# POC Operating Support Playbook

This is the lightweight operating-support model for the first five Organizational Capacity
Platform POCs. Support records are operational product data, not diagnostic evidence.

## Support channel

Sponsors, participants, and advisors use the **POC support** form beneath the workspace. The form
creates a reference beginning with `POC-` and may be used even when authentication is the problem.
It requests only contact, organization, role, issue type, urgency, and a technical description.

Never ask a participant to paste diagnostic answers, interview excerpts, names of other
participants, or confidential organizational material into a support request. If such content is
submitted, restrict access, do not copy it into product-learning records, and treat it according to
the confidentiality incident procedure.

## Triage and response

| Classification | Example | Initial action | Target response |
|---|---|---|---|
| S0 — confidentiality or tenant safety | Possible cross-client access, identity-response linkage, threshold bypass | Stop the affected workflow, preserve evidence, restrict access, and begin incident review | Immediate |
| S1 — method or decision integrity | Unsupported finding, missing uncertainty, evidence trace failure | Block release and assign advisor review | Same business day |
| S2 — completion blocker | Sign-in failure, invitation failure, lost draft, submission failure | Reproduce, provide a documented recovery path, and record staff assistance | Within one business day |
| S3 — comprehension | User cannot identify the next step or understand an instruction | Clarify the current step and record the friction for cohort review | Within two business days |
| S4 — preference | Non-blocking cosmetic or workflow preference | Record for later pattern review | During cohort review |

## Recovery boundaries

- Resend or revoke invitations through the sponsor workspace; never create an unmanaged account.
- Do not ask for passwords, authentication codes, recovery links, API keys, or encryption keys.
- Do not impersonate a participant or submit answers on their behalf.
- Preserve saved drafts when possible. A destructive recovery step requires explicit confirmation.
- Record whether staff intervention was required in the appropriate POC checkpoint feedback.
- Use the product change-candidate ledger only for de-identified operational patterns—not support
  messages containing identity or diagnostic content.

## Daily operating rhythm during an active POC

1. Review new POC support submissions and active advisor assignments each business day.
2. Acknowledge blocked or confidentiality-classified requests first.
3. Check invitation acceptance and aggregate completion without requesting participant answers.
4. Confirm evidence-review and leadership-release gates remain enforced.
5. Close the loop with the requester using the case reference.
6. Include counts, severity, recovery effort, and repeated friction in the final POC debrief.

## Completion standard

Lightweight support is functioning when every user has a visible request path, each request receives
a case reference, S0–S4 triage and response ownership are explicit, recovery preserves privacy and
diagnostic integrity, and support incidence can be reflected in the POC scorecard and debrief.
