# Organizational Capacity Model v0.1

## Core thesis
Growth creates complexity faster than organizational capacity evolves. When that happens, strategy does not necessarily fail. Execution flow degrades.

## Definition
Organizational Capacity is the ability of an organization to absorb complexity and convert it into coordinated action without excessive friction, value leakage, or leadership overload.

## Why it matters
Organizations usually notice capacity problems late because the early signals look ordinary: more meetings, slower decisions, unclear ownership, overloaded leaders, and technology projects that do not translate into execution leverage.

## Five-layer model
1. Business Strategy — what the organization is trying to accomplish.
2. Leadership System — how leaders create clarity, make tradeoffs, and manage ambiguity.
3. Organizational Capacity — the system's ability to metabolize complexity.
4. Execution Flow — how work, information, decisions, and ownership move.
5. Business Outcomes — the visible results of system health or system drag.

## Six capacity domains
- Leadership
- Decision Velocity
- Operating Rhythm
- Alignment
- Technology
- Culture

## Capacity signals
Capacity signals are recurring symptoms that indicate the system may be constrained: decision drag, meeting debt, unclear ownership, leadership overload, change fatigue, rework, stalled AI adoption, and value leakage.

## Diagnostic premise
Everyone has a prescription. Very few have a diagnosis. The diagnostic identifies where capacity is constrained before leaders invest in another intervention.
