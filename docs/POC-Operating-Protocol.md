# Organizational Capacity Platform POC Operating Protocol

## Purpose

The proof of concept validates whether the Organizational Capacity Platform can run one complete,
governed diagnostic cycle with real organizational context and real participants. It is not a demo,
free consulting engagement, intervention-delivery engagement, or promise that the initial
method and workflow are production-final.

Every POC uses the same diagnostic method available to a paid client. A no-charge POC entitlement
replaces diagnostic payment; it does not bypass confidentiality, evidence review, advisor review,
or leadership validation. The POC releases the governed finding and prioritized **Intervention
Directions**, then stops. Detailed intervention design, commercial acceptance, delivery,
30/60/90-day operating support, and reassessment require separate paid activation.

Every initial POC is steward-governed and follows the self-guided sponsor journey, scope rules, dual-output
contract, and release controls in `docs/Stewarded-POC-Operating-Model.md`.

## Questions the POC must answer

1. Can a sponsor establish enough context and perspective coverage to launch the diagnostic?
2. Do participants understand confidentiality and provide sufficiently concrete responses?
3. Does de-identification protect contributors without stripping the evidence of meaning?
4. Does synthesis identify a defensible organizational constraint, uncertainty, and competing
   explanations that expert review considers faithful to the evidence?
5. Does the governed finding help leadership make a better intervention decision?
6. Do the Intervention Directions explain what should change, why it fits the evidence, and what
   paid intervention design would need to resolve without giving away delivery?
7. Would the sponsor pay for the diagnostic and, separately, activate paid intervention design
   and delivery?

## Cohort and scope

- Use three to five POC organizations before declaring launch readiness.
- Prefer variation in size, operating model, diagnostic route, and presenting constraint.
- Each organization names one accountable sponsor and five to ten approved participant
  perspectives. The sponsor supplies framing context and does not consume a participant slot.
- Participant selection is not limited to leaders or the sponsor's reporting line. Executives,
  managers, individual contributors, peers, and cross-functional colleagues may participate when
  their work gives them relevant proximity to the diagnostic question.
- The platform visibly distinguishes the POC ceiling from the full diagnostic's capacity of up to
  fifty participants.
- The sponsor agrees to complete leadership validation and a final debrief. Intervention delivery
  is explicitly outside the POC and is not required to validate the diagnostic product.
- Senger Advisory records the delivery route, participant-plan rationale, accepted coverage
  gaps, timeline, and any manual assistance required.

## POC stages and exit gates

### 1. Enrollment and baseline

Record the sponsor's triggering concern, decision at risk, expected value, current alternatives,
and willingness-to-pay hypothesis. Confirm the privacy brief, approved participant coverage,
POC entitlement, target dates, and named decision maker.

Exit gate: the sponsor understands the distinction between the assessment, diagnostic, and
intervention and accepts the confidentiality and participation model.

### 2. Discovery and participant design

Guide the sponsor through six plain-language context prompts, with optional steward assistance
available without blocking progression. Record the sponsor's role, level, function, decision authority, and whether the organizational system
being examined is an enterprise, business unit, function, leadership layer, or cross-functional
system. The sponsor then approves perspective coverage, records any accepted coverage gaps, and
reviews the common 18-question contextualized protocol. The assigned steward finalizes the protocol;
the sponsor cannot directly edit or add questions.

Exit gate: the protocol can answer the leadership decision without becoming leading, bespoke to
one person's theory, or impossible to complete in approximately sixty minutes. No opening or design
meeting is required; the Revelation Session remains the required human gate before release.

### 3. Confidential participation

Invite the approved cohort and observe invitation acceptance, privacy acknowledgement, draft
recovery, completion, abandonment, questions, and support requests. Sponsor views remain limited
to invitation and aggregate completion status.

Exit gate: the confidentiality threshold is met, all required perspective slots are resolved,
and no individual response is exposed to the sponsor.

### 4. Evidence preparation and synthesis

Review de-identification quality, disclosure risk, excluded evidence, contradictions, competing
explanations, blind spots, confidence, and traceability. Compare automated synthesis with the
advisor's independent judgment before release.

Exit gate: the advisor approves the synthesis as faithful to the protected evidence, with
uncertainty preserved, and the locked Executive Capacity Brief and private Steward Revelation Guide
are ready for separate audiences. Leadership has not yet received the finding.

### 5. Leadership validation and Intervention Directions

Notify the sponsor that findings are ready and require a 30-minute Revelation Session. The Brief
remains locked until the steward deliberately releases it shortly before or during that session.
Using the private Revelation Guide, the steward explains the finding, uncertainty, organizational
mechanics, business implications, cost of inaction, and multiple weighted, evidence-informed
Intervention Directions. Leadership accepts the finding or requests revision. The output may
identify capabilities or design questions the paid intervention must address, but it does not
include a detailed implementation plan, training content, coaching, facilitation, specialist
sourcing, operating cadence, or reassessment.

Exit gate: leadership can explain the diagnosed constraint, why it is believed, what remains
uncertain, and why the Intervention Directions are relevant. The platform clearly identifies the
paid activation boundary, preserves the sponsor's released Brief, and keeps the Revelation Guide
private. During the POC, the sponsor may view and download the Brief for 30 days after release. A
paid diagnostic or intervention entitlement converts sponsor access to permanent; absent conversion,
online access and platform download end when the window expires, while any copy already downloaded
by the sponsor remains theirs.

### 6. Debrief and commercial decision

Conduct separate sponsor, participant, advisor, and internal operating debriefs. Record what
worked, where trust or comprehension broke down, what required manual intervention, and what the
sponsor would buy next.

Exit gate: the POC scorecard is complete, issues are triaged, and the cohort receives a written
continue, remediate, or stop decision.

## Feedback loops

Collect feedback at the moment of use, not only in a final retrospective:

| Moment | Primary respondent | Evidence collected |
|---|---|---|
| Sponsor setup | Sponsor | Clarity, effort, missing context, decision relevance |
| Participant onboarding | Participant | Trust, privacy comprehension, invitation friction |
| Interview completion | Participant | Effort, question clarity, psychological safety, technical issues |
| Evidence review | Advisor | De-identification quality, traceability, missing evidence |
| Finding release | Sponsor and advisor | Fidelity, surprise, uncertainty, actionability |
| Intervention Directions | Sponsor and advisor | Relevance, prioritization, value, route fit, willingness to pay |
| Final debrief | All roles separately | Trust, value, failure points, recommendation likelihood |

Do not collect participant-identifiable feedback inside diagnostic evidence. Participant product
feedback must remain operational data, separated from interview content and sponsor reporting.
The platform stores checkpoint feedback without a respondent identity or interview/evidence
reference. Participant feedback is never individually visible to the sponsor and never asks
participants about purchase intent.

## Issue taxonomy and severity

- **S0 — Safety or confidentiality:** cross-tenant exposure, identity-response linkage, threshold
  bypass, unauthorized access, or misleading confidentiality claim. Stop the affected workflow
  immediately; do not continue the cohort until contained and reviewed.
- **S1 — Method or decision integrity:** evidence cannot be traced, synthesis materially distorts
  the interviews, required uncertainty is omitted, or leadership receives an unsupported claim.
  Block release or intervention progression until remediated.
- **S2 — Completion or workflow:** a reasonable user cannot complete a critical step, loses work,
  receives no confirmation, or requires undocumented staff intervention. Prioritize before the
  next cohort unless a safe, documented workaround exists.
- **S3 — Comprehension or usability:** terminology, hierarchy, navigation, or instructions cause
  hesitation but do not block completion or trust. Bundle into the next validated usability pass.
- **S4 — Preference:** aesthetic or workflow preference without observed effect on safety,
  completion, comprehension, or decision quality. Record but do not automatically implement.

## Change-control rule

No POC comment becomes a product change by itself. Each proposed change records:

1. the observed problem and affected role;
2. the evidence and frequency across cohorts;
3. the risk of not changing;
4. the proposed product or method change;
5. the owner and target release;
6. the validation measure and rollback condition; and
7. whether the change affects method version, protocol version, confidentiality policy, data
   contract, migration, training, buyer language, or pricing.

Make immediate changes only for S0 issues or clearly reversible S1/S2 defects. For other issues,
prefer evidence across at least two users or organizations before changing the shared method.
Never weaken a confidentiality control merely to reduce friction.

The platform implements this rule as an append-only change-candidate ledger. The original observed
problem, operational evidence summary, severity, risk, proposed change, owner, target release,
validation measure, rollback condition, and governance impacts remain intact. A separate immutable
decision approves, rejects, or defers the candidate. Only approved candidates may receive validation
results, and each validation records whether the change was validated, invalidated, or inconclusive,
whether rollback was triggered, and what happens next. Participant identity, answers, interview
excerpts, and diagnostic evidence are prohibited from this product-learning record.
Deferral is non-terminal: it preserves the reason and may later be followed by approval or rejection.
Approval and rejection are terminal for that candidate.

## Launch-readiness thresholds

The initial thresholds are hypotheses to calibrate across the first cohort set:

- zero unresolved S0 issues;
- zero unresolved S1 issues affecting released findings;
- at least 80% of invited, accepted participants complete the diagnostic;
- at least 80% of completers say the confidentiality model was clear enough to answer candidly;
- at least 80% of completed interviews require no staff recovery or technical intervention;
- advisor fidelity rating of at least 4/5 for the governed synthesis in every launch-candidate POC;
- sponsor actionability rating of at least 4/5 for the governed finding and Intervention Directions;
- every released theme is traceable to approved de-identified evidence;
- every Intervention Direction is linked to the finding and states intended outcome, rationale,
  sequencing, uncertainty, and risk of inaction without including paid delivery assets;
- at least two sponsors indicate credible willingness to pay at the proposed diagnostic price;
- at least one sponsor indicates credible willingness to continue into paid intervention access;
- median manual support is low enough to support the intended automated-route economics.

Failure to meet a threshold does not automatically invalidate the product. It creates a documented
continue, remediate, narrow-scope, reprice, or stop decision.

## Cohort decision

At the end of each POC, assign one decision:

- **Continue:** no blocking issue; proceed with the next cohort under the same method version.
- **Continue with monitored adjustment:** make a bounded change with an explicit validation test.
- **Hold:** stop new cohorts until an S0, S1, or material S2 issue is resolved.
- **Narrow scope:** restrict route, customer type, use case, or claim until additional evidence exists.
- **Ready to monetize:** launch thresholds are met, commercial assumptions are credible, support
  requirements are understood, and production security and commerce gates are satisfied.

The cohort decision, supporting scorecard, approved changes, rejected suggestions, and unresolved
risks become part of the product decision history.

The assigned advisor records each cohort decision as an immutable entry. An ongoing decision must
name a future review date; a monitored adjustment must name at least one required change; and a hold
must name at least one unresolved risk. The platform does not infer or automatically award
`Ready to monetize`. That judgment remains explicit, evidence-backed, and auditable.

The advisor console may present an assignment-scoped aggregate scorecard. It may include counts,
completion rate, rating averages, support incidence, evidence-review totals, intervention state,
review count, advisor escalation count, and sponsor purchase-intent counts. It must not include
participant identity, participant feedback text, interview content, or diagnostic evidence text.
