# Experience North Star

The public site and authenticated Organizational Capacity Platform must make every visitor,
sponsor, participant, steward, and operator feel:

> I always know where I am, why this matters, what I need to do, and what happens next.

## Experience sequence

Every experience should move through the same decision logic:

1. **Recognize yourself.** Begin with the urgent operating condition in the user's language.
2. **See what it is costing.** Explain the credible, compounding consequence without false urgency.
3. **Understand what may be happening.** Introduce the Capacity Lens without prematurely diagnosing.
4. **Take the right next step.** Present one primary action and explain what it will and will not produce.
5. **Verify before advancing.** Show what the system heard, why the next action was selected, what
   remains uncertain, and how the user can review or revise safely.

## Design principles

- Sell the organizational problem before explaining the product or service.
- Use recognizable operating evidence to answer, “Is this us?”
- Create urgency from compounding execution cost, not countdowns, fear, or unsupported ROI claims.
- Prefer controlled contextual adaptation over novelty or ungoverned generative interfaces.
- Preserve one obvious primary action while allowing deliberate exploration.
- Use narrated reasoning wherever automation interprets, recommends, or advances work.
- Treat multimodal, predictive, and highly adaptive behavior as future capabilities that must earn
  their place through validated user benefit and governed data.

This document is the governing UX standard for future public-site and platform development.
