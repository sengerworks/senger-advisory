Northstar Services · Active intervention

Decision authority has not evolved with organizational complexity.

Routine decisions return to senior leaders because authority and information expectations remain implicit. The organization is testing whether explicit thresholds restore operating ownership before adding structure.

Confidence Moderate Validated with leadership
01 · What did we diagnose?

Routine decision ownership collapses upward under ambiguity.

The issue is not simply slow decisions. The operating system lacks explicit authority and information thresholds for recurring cross-functional decisions.

02 · Why do we believe it?

Four evidence themes converge.

Escalation recurs across functions; leaders and operating teams differ on whether missing authority or missing information is primary.

03 · What did we decide?

Test authority before adding structure.

Define and use decision rights and minimum information standards for three high-volume approvals over 90 days.

04 · What should change now?

Routine approvals should move without senior rescue.

  • Decision owners act within published thresholds.
  • Escalations include the required evidence.
  • Senior leaders spend less time routing routine work.
05 · What needs attention?

Information readiness may be the stronger constraint.

Two observations suggest teams escalate because inputs arrive incomplete. The next review must distinguish authority friction from information friction.

Diagnostic Record

Evidence before prescription.

Leadership accepted this hypothesis as a sound basis for a bounded intervention—not as certainty or proof of causality.

Executive finding

Decision authority and information expectations have not evolved with the volume of cross-functional work, causing routine ambiguity to consume senior attention.

Why now

A second-market launch will increase the number and consequence of decisions that cross functional boundaries.

Strategic exposure

Leadership attention may become the limiting resource for both market entry and existing client delivery.

Competing explanation

Information quality—not authority—may explain more of the observed delay than the primary hypothesis suggests.

Blind spot

The participant plan did not include one frontline delivery perspective.

Why moderate confidence

Multiple themes support escalation as a pattern, but mixed evidence preserves a credible alternative explanation.

Evidence themes

Routine ambiguity travels upwardConvergent · Leadership + Decisions

Operating teams describe unclear thresholds; leaders describe receiving decisions that should be resolved lower in the system.

Information arrives unevenlyMixed · Decisions + Technology

Some decisions stall despite clear ownership because supporting information is incomplete or differently interpreted.

Meetings compensate for unclear pathwaysConvergent · Operating Rhythm

Recurring coordination forums increasingly resolve individual decisions instead of improving the system that produces them.

Execution succeeds where boundaries are explicitMinority signal · Alignment

Teams with clear commercial thresholds move quickly, providing useful disconfirming evidence against a broad leadership explanation.

Accepted Intervention

Test authority before adding structure.

Scope and terms accepted
Objective

Reduce unnecessary escalation by clarifying authority and required information for three recurring decision types.

Duration

90 days with 30-, 60-, and 90-day decisions.

Service route

Platform-guided with human escalation available.

  1. First 30 daysMake the pathways explicit.

    Document owners, thresholds, required evidence, and escalation conditions for three decision types.

  2. Days 31–60Observe the operating system.

    Track how decisions move, where information fails, and when senior intervention remains necessary.

  3. Days 61–90Adapt and reassess.

    Revise the working hypothesis, complete the cycle, and test whether capacity—not merely activity—changed.
